Inside an assessment.

A guide to working with GreyCore: what we need, how we test, and what your team receives.

Agree the scope Test the system Document the findings Verify the fixes

Agree the scope.

We start with the application, API, infrastructure, or codebase you need assessed. Together, we define the targets, access, testing window, and restrictions before testing begins.

The scope determines the work and the fixed quote. An NDA and written authorization come before access to your systems.

What to send us
  • The systems in scope and a short description of how they are used.
  • Your stack, hosting environment, and available technical documentation.
  • The user roles and integrations that need testing.
  • Your deadline, any audit requirements, and known testing restrictions.

You receiveA written scope, testing schedule, rules of engagement, and quote.

Test the system.

We map the agreed attack surface, then test the authentication, authorization, data flows, and business logic that matter to the system.

Automated checks support manual investigation. We validate suspected issues and their impact within the agreed boundaries. Critical findings are raised during the engagement so your team can begin remediation.

How testing changes with the target
  • Web and API: permissions across roles and tenants, session handling, input processing, and application workflows.
  • Mobile: client behavior, local storage, communications, and the connected API.
  • Cloud: identity permissions, exposed services, configuration, and access to sensitive resources.
  • Source code: authentication flows, input-to-sink paths, framework usage, and business logic.

You receiveProgress updates and early notification of critical findings, with a direct line to the tester.

Document the findings.

The report separates the summary for decision makers from the technical detail engineers need to reproduce and fix each issue.

Findings include the affected components, severity, demonstrated impact, reproduction steps, and remediation guidance. The report records the scope and limitations of the assessment.

What is in the report
  • An executive summary of the assessment and the risks identified.
  • Technical findings with evidence and reproducible proof of concept.
  • Severity ratings and relevant weakness classifications.
  • Remediation recommendations for the engineering team.
  • A record of what was tested and any constraints on coverage.

You receiveAn executive summary, technical report, and letter of attestation for the assessment.

Request a redacted sample report

Verify the fixes.

Your engineers make the changes. We retest the reported issues to check whether the fixes address the original behavior and document anything still unresolved.

One-time assessments include a 30-day retest window. The retest covers reported findings; new features or a materially changed scope can require a separate assessment.

What a retest confirms

The updated report records each finding as resolved, partially resolved, or still open, based on the evidence from retesting. It describes the tested scope at that point in time. It does not certify that every possible vulnerability has been found.

You receiveUpdated finding status and evidence of the fixes verified during the retest.
